# Pair 1 on 5x5: three seeds on the `teacher-b5-1000k` pool

Results of the pair-1 comparison (`b5c192nbt-fson-mish-rvglr-bnh` convnet vs
`b5c192h3nbttfrs-fson-silu-rsnh` transformer, see [../../README.md](../../README.md))
trained on a fixed 5x5 pool generated by the `gen` stage, for seeds 1, 2 and 3.
Each seed is one `--stage all` run of the pipeline; the two models of a seed train
in parallel on one H100 each and see the same file order.

```bash
modal run --detach experiments/modal_pair1/app.py --stage all --data-source teacher --board-size 5 \
  --dataset teacher-b5-1000k --seed 1 --batch-size 2048 --max-samples 20000000 --samples-per-epoch 500000 \
  --epochs-per-export 2 --lr-schedule "(0,8.0),(10M,4.0),(14M,2.0),(17M,1.0),(19M,0.5)" \
  --max-val-samples 50000 --visits 100 --games-per-bot 400
```

| | |
|---|---|
| Pool | 1,048,600 rows from 88,000 self-play games by `kata1-b18c384nbt-s9996604416` at 200 visits (50 on cheap searches, cheap probability 0.5), `pos_len` 5, 4 L4 shards, 23 minutes, about $2.1 ([dataset.json](dataset.json)); 985,600 training rows in 14 files and one 63,000-row validation file. |
| Training | batch 2048, 20M samples (about 20 passes over the pool), 500K samples per epoch, export every 2 epochs (20 checkpoints per run), 50K validation rows per epoch, Muon, `-no-compile -use-bf16`, LR schedule `(0,8.0),(10M,4.0),(14M,2.0),(17M,1.0),(19M,0.5)`. |
| Evaluation | 7 log-spaced checkpoints per run, 14-bot round-robin, 100 visits, 400 games per bot (2,800 games), komi 25, area scoring, fp32 inference on one L4, Bayes Elo from `summarize_sgfs.py`. No anchor nets, so Elo is relative within each seed's eval only. |

## Files

`s<seed>/` holds, per seed: `report.md` and `report.json` from the report stage,
`elo.txt` and `elo.json` from the eval, `match.cfg` and `bots.json` for the match,
and for each run `<run>.summary.json` (train.py wall time, final validation,
exports), `<run>.metrics_val.json` (one line per epoch) and
`<run>.metrics_train.json` (one line per 250 batches). The sgf files (64 per eval,
about 1.2 MB) stay on the volume:

```bash
modal volume get katago-pair1 eval/pair1-b5-s1-v100/sgfs ./sgfs-s1
```

`../../cross_seed.py` produced the cross-seed tables below:

```bash
python experiments/modal_pair1/cross_seed.py experiments/modal_pair1/results/teacher-b5-1000k 1 2 3
```

## Reading

- **Elo is a tie from 4M samples onward in every seed.** The mean tf-minus-conv
  differences at checkpoints 4 to 7 are +1, +3, +12 and +7 Elo, all inside the
  pooled three-seed error of about 15. The transformer's lean at 12M (+11, +13,
  +11) is the most consistent signal but is not significant and does not grow by
  20M.
- **The 3M checkpoint is volatile, not a family effect.** Its difference swings
  from -97 to +75 across seeds. Both nets are still 50 to 100 Elo below their
  plateau there with the LR at its 8x peak, so which net has a systematic hole in
  a solved line at that moment is luck.
- **Both curves flatten by 4M to 5M samples.** In all six runs the last
  checkpoint-to-checkpoint Elo gain larger than one standard error is at 3M to
  5.2M samples, and every later checkpoint sits within 25 Elo of the final one.
- **The policy-loss ordering is the same in every seed.** The convnet is ahead at
  1M (by 0.09 to 0.23), the transformer is ahead from 2M through 12M (mean -0.025
  shrinking to -0.008), and the end is a dead heat (mean +0.0004, seeds split).
  The transformer's validation p0loss bottoms at 17.4M to 17.5M samples in all
  three seeds and rises 0.001 to 0.002 afterwards while its training loss keeps
  falling; its train/val gap is 0.027 against 0.008 for the convnet. The convnet
  is still at or within 0.0004 of its minimum at 20M.
- **Value loss** favors the transformer by 0.002 to 0.003 through mid-training and
  ties at the end. **Top-1 accuracy** is the one metric that stays separated:
  0.7483 against 0.7426 at the end, about three seed standard deviations apart.
- **What it says.** On 5x5 the two families reach the same strength and the same
  losses. The transformer gets there with more memorization of the pool and a
  slightly sharper argmax policy, at no strength cost. A 3x3 convolution covers a
  5x5 board after two blocks, so the local-versus-global distinction the comparison
  is about is mostly absent here; this run validates the pipeline, the batch and
  LR settings and the convergence-detection protocol rather than the architecture
  question. 9x9 is the smallest size where that distinction survives.

## Notes

- Seed 1's first eval was interrupted when Modal preempted the orchestrator
  container after 950 games and restarted it with the same input. The restart
  skipped data, resumed training as a no-op and skipped export as designed, but
  its second match appended a second set of sgf files into the same eval directory,
  which would have folded the first attempt's games into the Elo. `run_match` now
  clears the sgf directory before each match (commit 919b67e2); the app was
  stopped and eval and report were re-run cleanly (2,800 games, 64 sgf files,
  394 to 404 games per bot). Seeds 2 and 3 saw no preemption.
- Match speed varies by host: seed 2's 2,800 games took 42 minutes against 29 and
  31 for the others. In every match the convnet checkpoints averaged 2 to 4 s per
  move against 0.5 to 1.1 s for the transformer checkpoints (fp32 CUDA inference,
  64 game threads on one L4), which is why eval dominates wall-clock here.
- Seed 3's training logged a 41st epoch (20,402,176 samples) after its last export
  at 19,914,752, so its final validation and its last checkpoint differ slightly.
